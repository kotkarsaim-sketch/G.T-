Animal Control By-law No. 2023-047

Being a by-law of The Corporation of the City of Rivermont respecting the keeping, licensing and control of animals. Passed 2023-11-06, effective 2024-01-01.

1. Definitions
In this By-law: "dwelling unit" means any building or part of a building used as a permanent residence and includes a basement suite occupied as a separate residence; "owner" includes a person who keeps, harbours or has custody of an animal; "livestock" includes horses, cattle, swine, sheep, goats and poultry; "bees" means honeybees of the genus Apis kept in managed hives.

2. Licensing of dogs
Every owner of a dog older than three months shall obtain an annual dog licence on or before March 31. Fees are set in the annual fees schedule; a reduced fee applies to sterilized dogs and to service dogs, which are licensed without charge. An owner moving into the municipality shall obtain a licence within fourteen days.

3. Number limits - cats and dogs
No dwelling unit shall keep more than three dogs or more than four cats over the age of three months, and no more than five such animals in total, except under a multiple-animal permit issued by the Chief Administrative Officer under section 8. This limit does not apply to a veterinary clinic, animal shelter or licensed kennel.

4. Bees and urban hives
A person may keep bees on land zoned R-1 or R-2 only if all of the following are met: no more than two hives per lot; hives are at least five metres from any property line and ten metres from any public sidewalk; a continuous water source is maintained within ten metres of the hives; and hive entrances face away from adjoining dwelling units. Beekeeping in all other zones requires written permission from the municipality. See also the Noise Control By-law, section 5, which exempts compliant beekeeping from its general prohibitions.

5. Prohibited animals
No person shall keep livestock in any residential zone except on a lot of at least 0.8 hectares, and nowhere within the municipality shall any person keep: any member of the order Carnivora other than a domestic dog or cat; venomous reptiles; or any wildlife species protected under provincial regulation.

6. Care standards
An owner shall provide each animal with adequate food, water, shelter and veterinary care, and shall not leave an animal unattended in a motor vehicle where ambient conditions present a risk to its health. Contravention of this section may result in seizure of the animal under provincial animal protection legislation.

7. Nuisance and control
An owner whose animal barks, howls or otherwise persistently disturbs any person commits an offence for the purposes of the Noise Control By-law where the disturbance exceeds fifteen minutes. Dogs must be leashed in public places other than designated off-leash areas, and an owner shall immediately remove and dispose of excrement deposited by the animal on public or private property other than their own.

8. Multiple-animal permits
The Chief Administrative Officer may issue a permit authorizing numbers exceeding section 3 where the applicant demonstrates adequate enclosure space, waste management and, where applicable, sound mitigation. Permits are valid for twenty-four months and may include conditions.

9. Offences and penalties
A person who contravenes this By-law is liable to a fixed penalty of $200 for a first offence and $400 for each subsequent offence within twenty-four months, or to prosecution with fines up to $5,000 for continuing contravention.
